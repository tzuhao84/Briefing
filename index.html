How to read this
  • Start with the summary line. It tells you whether it was a broad move (most stocks the same way) or a few stocks moving on their own.
  • Today's moves: bars to the right rose, bars to the left fell; longer means a bigger move. All markets share one scale, so bar lengths compare across Taiwan and the US.
  • Compare with the grey line, not just with zero. It marks the benchmark (TAIEX, S&P 500). A stock that fell less than the benchmark held up better than the market.
  • One day is mostly noise; the 3-month shape is the story. Each mini-chart has its own scale, so read its direction and steepness, not its height against its neighbours.
  • The range bar shows where today's price sits between the 3-month low (left) and high (right). Near the high means momentum; near the low after a long slide can mean weakness or a bargain. Check the headlines before deciding which.
  • Touch or hover a mini-chart to read the price on any day, or a bar for the price and how it did against the benchmark.
